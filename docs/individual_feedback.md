# Individual Feedback

## Keith Coughlin

What made this capstone feel different from a normal course project is that somewhere in the middle of it I realized I was building something I could actually use at work. My day job puts me close to problems in early cancer detection and treatment, and the more time I spent in the omics, scoring, and LLM-scoring modules, the more I saw how the same evidence-synthesis pattern we built for CRC biomarkers could be pointed at the real decisions my team makes about which signals to follow up on. The capstone stopped being an academic exercise and started being a prototype. With the right validation panel and the right target indication, it could realistically contribute to identifying cancer earlier in patients and informing how it gets treated. Knowing that downstream impact was possible changed how I approached every part of the build. I took the "explainable" requirement seriously not because the rubric asked me to, but because I could picture handing the ranked list to a clinician or a scientist at my company and needing to defend every weight, threshold, and LLM prompt on its merits. Every design choice had to be traceable to a config file or a documented rubric, never buried in code. My specific contributions were the omics differential-expression module, the LLM scoring stream, the weighted scoring and ranking logic, the supervised ML feature-importance classifier, the overall pipeline orchestration, and the integration of Ayan's MMR-deficiency classifier into the repo so it runs alongside the main pipeline. If I could offer one suggestion for the course, it would be a more structured mid-semester checkpoint focused specifically on validation strategy. Our team would have pivoted earlier and arrived at a stronger result with less rework. Overall, the experience of taking a real AI system from an empty repository to something I'd genuinely trust to hand to a collaborator, and to a use case I actually care about, made this the most valuable course I've taken in the program.
